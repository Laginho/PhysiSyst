# Ropes are our own constraint, solved around `world.step()`

A rope is not a Rapier joint. The simulator solves it itself, once per step, in the hook that already applies the applied forces. One mechanism covers every rope over ideal pulleys, or none; a rope over a pulley with mass (PHY-25) runs the same prediction and correction once per **piece** of rope, solved together (below). Ropes sharing a dynamic body form a connected group (PHY-41): `step()` solves all its pieces together in prediction and correction. A rope without massive pulleys contributes one piece. An isolated rope keeps its scalar or per-piece path, and shared fixed anchors do not join groups.

## Why not Rapier's rope joint

- Rapier 0.20 has no pulley joint. A rope over a pulley constrains a **sum** of distances (`L₁ + L₂ = const`), and no Rapier joint can express that. Its rope joint only caps the distance between two points.
- Rapier returns no force from any joint. The readout panel needs `T`, and with our own constraint `T` falls out of the calculation.
- One mechanism for every rope keeps the pendulum, pulleys in series and the movable pulley (PHY-24) on the same code path as the tracer.

## Mechanism

`L` is the rope's path length (tangent legs plus wrapped arcs, `ropePath`) at the **document** poses, computed when the world is built. Structural editing is allowed only at t = 0 (PHY-39); `replaceScene` always starts from the document.

The rope pulls at each of its points in path order: each end along its one leg, and each pulley along the sum of the leg that arrives and the leg that leaves — so a pulley on a dynamic body (a movable pulley) is pulled at its axle. Per unit tension that pull `u` is also minus the gradient of the path length at the point. Pulls that land on one body (a movable pulley and a rope end on the same body) are summed before the body's mass enters. Each step, for each rope:

1. **Before the step (prediction, by position).** Integrate each point's free motion over the step the way Rapier does — gravity and the forces already added, no contacts, no rope. Rapier splits the step into `n = world.numSolverIterations` substeps, so a constant acceleration moves a body `φ = (n + 1)/2n` of the Euler distance `Δt²·a` while its velocity still gains `Δt·a` in full (measured 5/8 at `n = 4`). The target is where the step should leave the rope: at `L` if it was slack (no pull if the free step stays short of `L`), or with 20% of any stretch pulled back. The tension is the one that moves the free end-of-step length onto that target, `T = (c_free − target) / (φ·Δt²·K)`, plus last step's residual, clamped at `T ≥ 0` and applied as a force so Rapier's contact and friction solve sees it.
   - The pull goes along the legs of the **mid-step** rope (every point halfway to its free end-of-step position). Legs turn while the step runs: along the old legs the pull misses the centripetal part of a swing, along the end-of-step legs it does work against the swing and drains ~2% of `v²` per step. Along the mid-step legs it is square to the chord each point travels.
   - `K = Σ J M⁻¹ J′ᵀ` over bodies is asymmetric: `J` is the mid-step pull applied, `J′` the end-of-step length gradient it acts against. It can go negative, which reads as "no rope this step".
   - The prediction also records the reading a contact-free step should end with — the rate form of the same pull — so the residual carries only what contacts add.
2. **`world.step()`.**
3. **After the step (correction).** Contacts and friction made the prediction wrong. An impulse along the rope removes whatever lengthening rate is left beyond the rope's allowance, clamped so the total tension never goes negative (the rope never pushes). The tension this implies is the reading; its excess over the recorded prediction is the **residual** that warm-starts the next step. In `correctPieces`, a piece ending more than `ROPE_SLIP = 0.01 m` beyond the target stretch saved by `pullPieces` instead gets residual zero (PHY-52): a contact cancelled the pull, and carrying that error forward would store tension. This leaves the step's tension reading and correction impulse unchanged. The scalar `correctRope` deliberately keeps its original residual: applying this rule there makes the ideal 2/2 scene launched at vy = 4 gain +2.34 J.

Prediction and correction share one allowance (`ropeAllowance`): a slack rope may close its gap in one step, a stretched one must pull back 20% of the stretch. A correction that aimed at zero lengthening would undo the pull-back, and the warm start would cancel the next one.

The rope is unilateral: `T = 0` and `slack = true` whenever no pull is needed. The jerk that pulls a slack rope taut is in the prediction, so it never becomes a warm start (a rate prediction let it through and pulled the bodies 2 cm inside `L`). The warm start is what makes a rope exact under friction: without it, the block on the table drifts 1.4–2.7 mm off `L` in 1.5 s.

## Pulleys with mass (PHY-25)

A pulley with `mass > 0` is a disk the rope does not slip on, so it turns and the tension differs on each side.

- **The disk** is a Rapier body of its own: translation locked, gravity scale 0, a ball collider of mass `M` (so `I = ½MR²`) in collision group 0, touching nothing. `placeDisks` sets it onto its axle before every read of the rope. Mounted on a dynamic body, the pulley's mass also rides the mount as a second contact-less collider, `setMassProperties(M, 0, 0)` placed at the anchor: a point mass on the axle, which by parallel axes adds `M` to the mount's translation and `M·d²` to its inertia, and makes the pulley's weight act on the mount. `mass` absent or 0 builds no disk and no mass collider, but still gets the rim (below).
- **Grips and pieces.** Each pulley with mass on a rope's path is a **grip**; the grips cut the rope into **pieces**, each of fixed length taken at the document poses like `L`. A piece runs from an end or grip to the next grip or end, with any ideal pulleys inside it wrapped as before. A grip pulls its mount at the axle and its disk at the tangent point, both along the piece's one leg there: the axle takes the force, the disk the torque.
- **No slip, at position level.** Each grip keeps a **share**: the angle of its arc that belongs to the arriving piece, measured from where the rope meets the disk; the leaving piece holds the rest. The mark turns with the disk: `gripShares` adds the disk's turn since the last update and subtracts the meeting point's drift. The turn is integrated by the grip itself, never read from Rapier's `disk.rotation()`, whose angle runs short of `ω·Δt` over the substeps: it is `Δt·(ω₀ + φ·(ω₁ − ω₀))`, with `ω₀` captured in `pullPieces` (stored on the grip as `w0`), `ω₁` read after `world.step()` and before the correction impulses, and `φ` from `substepFactor`. `pullPieces` predicts it with `ω₁` extrapolated from the disk's torque, `correctPieces` applies it with the measured `ω₁`, so prediction and correction use the same integration. Rapier stays the owner of `ω`, except across the step: Rapier caps `|ω|·Δt` at π/4 on every body, so `step()` stores each disk's `ω`, sets the disk's angular velocity to 0 for `world.step()` (the solver then integrates only the torque's increment), and afterwards gives it the stored `ω` plus `angvel()` (PHY-50). Prediction and correction still read the whole `ω`, and the disk's angle in Rapier does not measure the grip's turn. Only the meeting point's drift goes through `wrapAngle`, so it may change by less than π per step. `pieceLengths` sums each piece's legs, the arcs inside it and its shares of the grips at its ends. The shares start mid-arc and catch up with the disks after every step in `correctPieces`.
- **Tensions solve together.** Prediction (`pullPieces`) and correction (`correctPieces`) are the scalar rope's, once per piece: the disk's free turn joins the free motion, and the allowance and 20% pull-back apply per piece. Two pieces couple through the disk they share and any body both pull, so `K` is a matrix, `Kₖₗ = J′ₖ M⁻¹ Jₗᵀ` (`ropeInvMass(pulls, along)`), and `tautTensions` solves `K(T − base) = b` with `solveLinear` (partial pivoting) as an active set: the piece with the least nonpositive `T` goes slack and the rest re-solve; once active tensions are positive, the slack piece with the largest positive residual `rhsₖ − Σₗ Kₖₗ Tₗ` rejoins and the system re-solves, including when the active set is empty (PHY-46). Residuals within `10⁻⁹·max(1, max |rhs|)` count as zero; `10n²` iterations cap possible cycling for asymmetric `K`, returning the last tensions clamped nonnegative at the cap. `RopeState.tension` is the largest piece's, `slack` only when every piece is; `segments` gives each leg its piece's `T`.
- **Torques.** `resetForces` does not clear torques, and nothing but the rope drives a disk, so `step()` clears the disks' torques every step as well (bodies: PHY-34).

## The rim and the kept wrap direction (PHY-45)

- **The rim.** Every pulley, ideal or with mass, gets one ball collider of radius `R` on its mount at the pulley's anchor, so a body that reaches the pulley stops instead of passing through it. It has mass, friction and restitution zero, both combine rules `Min`, and it keeps the default collision groups. It is the mount's own collider, so the mount never collides with its own rim; on a movable pulley the rim travels with the mount and can collide with other bodies. The rim is distinct from the mass colliders above (the disk's ball and the mount's point mass), which stay in group 0 and touch nothing.
- **The kept direction.** `RopeBinding.keep` holds one wrap direction per pulley, taken from the document poses when the world is built. `ropeFrame` hands it to `ropePath`, so a swing that flips the sign of the turn at a pulley does not flip its wrap (the two wraps only have the same length when the ends and the center are collinear, so any other switch jumps the length). `keep` is refreshed from the path at every read of the bodies' real poses; the predicted poses (mid and end of step) only read it. The editor at t = 0 draws `scenePath`, which keeps no direction: it follows the turn at the poses it is given. During playback the canvas draws the path the simulator solved (PHY-56): `RopeBinding.path` is set where `keep` is, and `readConstraints` exposes it as `RopeState.path`, so the kept direction and the unwound sweeps shape the drawing too and the tension arrows follow the same path.
- **The unwound sweep (PHY-54).** With the direction kept, a sweep that reached 0 and passed crossed the 0/2π seam and the length jumped by about 2πR, which the 20% pull-back and the warm start turned into hundreds of joules. Each ideal pulley now also holds its **unwound sweep** in `RopeBinding.sweeps`, seeded at the document poses and refreshed where `keep` is: `ropePath(a, b, pulleys, keep, sweeps)` takes the new one as the old plus `wrapAngle(raw − old)`, so it does not jump at the seam. A negative sweep means the rope has come loose: the path is the straight leg between the neighbours, the pulley sits on that leg as the joint of two collinear segments (CLEAN-25: the k-th of the n loose pulleys on a leg, in `via` order, at parameter t = k/(n + 1) of the leg whatever its center, so the segments stay collinear and the same way on and none shrinks to length 0 and stops being pulled, even when the centers' projections swap order, coincide or fall off an end), it pulls nothing and adds no length, and its sweep keeps being measured with the kept direction so the rope takes it back on the same side. With several pulleys the legs and sweeps of the engaged ones come from the construction over the engaged ones alone; a loose pulley's sweep is measured over the engaged ones plus itself. Above 2π the rope winds more than a turn and the length stays continuous. The predicted poses read the sweeps and do not update them, and a pulley without history (`scenePath`, which keeps no history) behaves as before. A rope with a pulley with mass gets no history on any pulley, since `pieceLengths` sums `R·sweep` of every arc inside a piece (clamped at 0 for the loose ones of a rope without a grip); the rope that comes off a pulley with mass joins two pieces into one and is PHY-55. The canvas draws the loose rope as the straight leg, with no arc for a negative sweep (PHY-56).
- **The guard in `pullPieces`.** When `ropeInvMass(row.pulls, row.along) <= 0` (contact holds a body, so the end-of-step leg pushes against the mid-step one), the row's `along` falls back to its `pulls` and the row stays in the matrix with a positive diagonal. The scalar `pullRope` branch `k <= 0` is a different thing: it zeroes the tension and returns.
- **No stored tension after a cancelled pull (PHY-52).** A massive-pulley rim impact used to accumulate residual while contact prevented the predicted pull, injecting +54.52 J into the 3/1 bodies and +3.03 J into the 1/2 bodies. `pullPieces` saves each piece's target stretch, including the single piece of an ideal rope in a connected group; `correctPieces` clears its residual when actual stretch exceeds that target by more than `ROPE_SLIP`. This applies to any contact that cancels the pull, not just the rim; the tension and correction impulse of the current step stay unchanged. The 1 cm threshold preserves friction warm starts (measured target error at most 0.68 mm, versus 42–94 mm at impacts). It assumes meter-scale scenes and can miss impacts below 1 cm; a threshold relative to predicted step displacement is the next option if those scales are needed. `correctRope` stays outside this rule because of the +2.34 J ideal 2/2, vy = 4 regression.

The scalar path (`pullRope`/`correctRope`) stays separate for isolated ropes even though one piece is the scalar rope: PHY-25 criterion 4 wants `mass` absent or 0 to run bit for bit like PHY-24, and folding the scalar rope into the matrix path would reorder its arithmetic. The duplication between `pullPieces`/`pullRope` and `correctPieces`/`correctRope` is that price. For a connected group, `pullPieces` and `correctPieces` flatten every rope's pieces into one system: the cross-rope entries of `K` come from the same `ropeInvMass`, and `tautTensions` clamps each piece independently. All free predictions are read before any tension force is applied, and all correction rates before any impulse is applied. Each rope retains its own tension, residual and segment readings. Dynamic pulley mounts and shared disks also connect ropes; grouping is rebuilt each step, including after `replaceScene`.

Redundant constraints, such as two collinear ropes supporting the same particle, make `K` singular without making support impossible. If elimination encounters a pivot below `10⁻¹²` of the largest matrix entry, `solveLinear` retries once with `10⁻⁹` of that scale added to the diagonal; a zero matrix still returns no solution. The same fallback serves the contact-free prediction and both active-set solves. It adds tiny numerical compliance only to numerically singular systems, preserving support without requiring a unique division of tension between redundant ropes. A rank-revealing solve can replace it if exact redundant tensions become necessary; systems above the pivot threshold keep their existing arithmetic.

## Springs (PHY-26)

A spring is not a Rapier joint either. Its physical force is `F_el = k·Δx + c·ẋ`; `pushSpring` integrates it with implicit damping (below) and applies it along the axis between the anchors as a force of our own, `+u` on end `a` and `−u` on `b` (`u` from `a` to `b`), so a stretched spring pulls its ends together and damping opposes separation. A fixed end gets no force and counts as still.

- **Same hook, before the ropes.** The springs are pushed after the applied forces and before any rope's prediction, so the rope's free motion already includes them.
- **Same substep factor.** A constant force over Rapier's substeps moves a body only `φ` of the Euler distance while its velocity gains the full `Δt·F/m`; for a spring that alone pumps energy in. The force is taken with both ends moved `(1 − φ)Δt` ahead at their velocity now (`springAt`), which makes the step area-preserving for a linear spring. Without that lead all six oscillation tests of PHY-26 fail.
- **Implicit stiffness and damping (PHY-40, PHY-47).** The applied force is `F = [k·Δx_lead + c·ẋ + (k·lead + c)·δẋ] / [1 + (k·lead + c)·Δt·K]`, with `lead = (1 − φ)Δt` and `K = 1/m_ef` from `ropeInvMass` on the two opposite anchor pulls, including translation and rotational inertia; a fixed end contributes zero. `δẋ = ẋ_free − ẋ` projects the anchors' free velocity gain onto the spring axis, using `freePointVelocity` and `lengtheningRate` to include gravity, forces and torques already applied this step. Both terms use the end-of-step relative velocity `ẋ′ = ẋ_free − K·F·Δt`: the elastic term with its existing lead, damping with the full step. Springs, ideal or with mass (PHY-48, below), sharing dynamic bodies, transitively, form a connected group; sharing only a fixed body does not connect them. At the first member of each group in the existing hook, `pushSpring` reads the group's free predictions before adding any of its forces. For an ideal-only group, `solveLinear` solves `[I + diag(q)*dt*K]F = b`, where `q_i = k_i*lead + c_i` and b is the scalar numerator above for each spring. Mixed groups instead use the force-derivative couplings described below. `K_ij = ropeInvMass(pulls_j, pulls_i)` includes shared translation and rotation. Members keep document order within the matrix; subsequent calls for the group do nothing. Solving disconnected groups separately prevents one independent spring's large damping from triggering the solver's scale-based regularization in another group. This preserves equilibrium for opposing springs regardless of document order or damping: sequential free predictions incorrectly include only earlier spring forces, while a common snapshot with separate scalar denominators still unbalances springs with different damping. The identity term keeps the system invertible even when K is singular or zero. The single-spring equation above is unchanged. Omitting free acceleration divides the spring's static force by the denominator while leaving the external load intact, shifting equilibrium away from `load/k`; the correction preserves `mg/k` under gravity and `load/k` under a constant applied force. PHY-40 made only damping implicit, preventing velocity amplification when `c·Δt·K > 2`, but left the elastic force explicit. That returned overdamped motion too quickly (the slow time constant should approach `c/k`) and let light, stiff springs diverge when `√(k/m)·Δt > 2`. Making both terms implicit keeps the PHY-26 soft-spring period and amplitude within their existing tolerances; for `k = 400`, `c = 0` and `m = 0.01 / 0.05 / 0.1`, energy stays below `1.2·E₀` over 600 steps. It bounds stiff-spring energy without claiming accurate phase for oscillations unresolved at 60 Hz. With no projected free acceleration and `k·lead = 0` this reduces to PHY-40; springs with mass keep their θ-method chain (PHY-30), whose ends join the same solve (next item).
- **A spring with mass solves in the group (PHY-48).** The chain's interior is already implicit (θ-method), but its ends used to be prescribed at the velocity they have now and the resulting `F_a`, `F_b` applied to the bodies as explicit forces. Each chain link carries `(N + 1)·k` and `(N + 1)·c`, so with high `c`, or a stiff `k` against a light body, the end link is stiffer than an explicit step can take and the spring injected energy (8·10⁴ J from `c = 100` with `m = 1`, `k = 40`, `mₛ = 0.1`; 15 200 J for `m = 0.01`, `k = 400`, `mₛ = 0.001`). Now each dynamic end is a row of the group solve beside the ideal springs' rows. The chain step takes the end velocities `W` at the end of the step (end position `at + Δt·((1 − θ)·v + θ·W)`) and is affine in `W`; three runs (base, `W_a + 1`, `W_b + 1`) give `f₀` and `∂f/∂W` exactly, starting from `W` = `freePointVelocity` along the axis. The row is `F − (∂f/∂W)·Δt·Σ K·F = f₀`, with `K = ropeInvMass` between the rows as for ideal springs; a fixed end is no row. With the solved forces the chain runs once more at the end velocities they give; that run keeps `chain.p`, `chain.w` and `chain.force`, and the rebase after `world.step()` (PHY-42) stays. `CHAIN_THETA = 0.55` and the lag `(θ − 1 + φ)Δt` are unchanged. Ropes sharing a body with a spring stay outside the group. The two ends on the same body are covered by the structure of the solve, through the cross term of `K`: a spring on its own body only makes an internal force.
  - **Why not the chain alone (A).** Ends as a θ solve of mass `1/K`, one chain at a time, removes the blow-up, but each chain reads the body's free velocity after the springs before it already pushed. Two springs on one body then depend on document order: 30 mm of drift, and `PHY-47: chain and ideal spring stay balanced…` goes red. Reading every free velocity first removes the drift but blows up once the body is released (8·10⁴ J), because each spring acts as the body's only owner; reading the velocity now instead of the free one gets the hanging equilibrium wrong by 167%.
  - **Why not an implicit denominator on the end link (B)** (as PHY-47 does for ideal springs): it sees the link's `9c`, not the spring's `c`. It overdamps (−3.7% at step 600 with `c = 200`), breaks `F_b − F_a ≈ mₛ·a/2` of PHY-30, and drifts 56 mm with two springs.
- **The readout** (`readSpring`) is `F_el` at the current state, no lead: `dx` signed (+ stretched), the same `force` at both ends while the spring is massless.

Rapier's spring joint (`JointData.spring(x0, k, c, …)`) was tried first and missed the PHY-26 tolerances: a horizontal `m = 1`, `k = 40` spring lost 0.0297 m of a 0.1 m amplitude in 5 periods (0.002 allowed), the vertical equilibrium was off by 2.05% (2% allowed), and the damped cases found 4 and 2 peaks where 5 were needed. And, like every joint, it returns no force for the readout.

## Consequences

- Ropes on shared dynamic bodies solve together as one matrix per connected group, in both prediction and correction (PHY-41). Independent ropes, including ropes sharing only fixed anchors, keep their previous paths. The former sequential solve displaced a 1 kg particle suspended from anchors at (−1, 0.1) and (1, 0.1) by 32.99 mm over 300 steps; iterating correction still missed the tolerance, so it was replaced by the grouped solve. The regression requires displacement below 1 mm throughout, both tensions within 1% of 49.29 N from step 10, and neither rope slack.
- The correction projects the end-of-step chord velocity back onto the rope, and that projection drains a fast swing: ~4% of a 1 m loop's energy per turn at `v₀² = 6gL`. It is marked `ponytail:` in `correctRope` (and `correctPieces` points there); the upgrade is a RATTLE-style position-and-velocity projection, if energy readouts ever need it.
- `φ` is read from `world.numSolverIterations`; it assumes Rapier's substepping stays the one measured here (Rapier 0.20).
- Ropes have no collider: a rope passes through bodies, and it is drawn from the same `ropePath` the simulator solves on. A pulley does collide: its rim stops bodies that reach it. The colliders that only carry a pulley's mass stay in collision group 0 and touch nothing.
- The disk's turn is not wrapped, so `gripShares` has no per-step limit on it. The disk is not held to Rapier's cap on `|ω|·Δt` because it enters `world.step()` with `ω = 0` and gets its `ω` back afterwards (PHY-50). That works only for disks, which have no contacts (the rim sits on the mount, not the disk): ordinary bodies must turn inside Rapier for their contacts, so they keep the cap (PHY-51), and the mechanism removes no other integration limit of Rapier's.
- The spring's `(1 − φ)Δt` lead reads the same `substepFactor` as the rope prediction, so the assumption above covers both.
